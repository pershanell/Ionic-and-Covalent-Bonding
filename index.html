How the Ion Lab works

Group 1 and 2 metals: click their valence electrons to remove them.
Group 6 and 7 non-metals: click empty outer-shell positions to accept electrons.
Removed electrons are held temporarily in the electron bank at the top of the sandbox.
Once a complete outer shell is reached, that ion is locked so no extra electrons can be removed or added.
Form the ionic substance: bring completed positive and negative ions close together. Opposite charges snap together; like charges repel when released.
When the joined ions have an overall charge of zero, the lab confirms that the charges balance correctly.
Drag an ion onto the bin in the top-left of the sandbox to remove it. Any of its donated electrons still in the electron bank are removed as well.

The nucleus shows the element symbol and, underneath it, the number of protons. The ion charge changes as electrons are transferred.

    ⚛ Covalent Lab

    Drag atoms together — discover how they share electrons

    Atom Tray

    Click an atom to place it automatically in an open space in the workspace, then drag atoms near each other to bond. To remove an atom, drag it onto the bin in the top-left of the workspace. Click a formed bond to add another shared pair and turn it into a double or triple bond.

    Sandbox: build any molecule you like. There's no wrong answer here.

    Electron Key

    Gold ring = ready to bond (unpaired)
    Plain dot = non‑bonding (lone pair)
    Each element has its own electron colour — matching dots (or a dot + ×) sitting in the overlap show a shared pair.

    A full outer shell makes an atom stable — watch for its ring turning green. Bring two atoms close and their outer shells overlap to form a shared pair. Non-bonding electrons automatically move around the shell to stay away from bonding pairs and from one another. Let go and the molecule settles into its real shape. Drag an atom away to break its bond. Click a bond to add another shared pair (single → double → triple).
